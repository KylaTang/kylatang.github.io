Cohort Analysis

Something broke between 2025 and 2026.

2025 users retained at 88–100% in Month 1. 2026 new acquisitions: 40–50%. That's not a small dip — it's a structural shift. The most likely culprit is channel mix, not product.

Cohort Users M1 Retention Pattern Dominant Channel
2025 (Established) ~40 88–100% Repeat ordering holds — habitual reordering Organic + Referral
2026 (New) ~40 40–50% Half churn after first interaction Paid + Social
⚠️
Most Likely Cause: Channel Quality + No Onboarding Sequence
2026 new users arrive via paid/social (higher volume, lower intent) and face no structured onboarding. Without a 72h first-order nudge, ~50% of new acquisitions become Tier 3 — registered but never converted.

Chicago: High Spend, Low Satisfaction

Ask Data with One Natural-language Question

Question raised by user → SQL transferred automatically by LLM
SELECT
  user_id,
  COUNT(*)                        AS order_count,
  ROUND(AVG(order_total), 2)      AS avg_spend,
  ROUND(AVG(rating), 1)           AS avg_rating,
  GROUP_CONCAT(DISTINCT cuisine_type
               ORDER BY cuisine_type
               SEPARATOR ', ')    AS cuisines_ordered
FROM ubereats_mock
GROUP BY user_id
HAVING order_count >= 2
ORDER BY order_count DESC
LIMIT 8;

Auto-analysis — pattern + interpretation surfaced by LLM
Pattern: Top 3 users all ordered Burgers or Salads — no outlier cuisines. High-frequency behaviour clusters around familiar, low-decision categories.
Interpretation: Repeat ordering is habitual, not exploratory. Active users are not using the platform to discover new cuisines — they're reordering comfort foods.
Follow-up worth asking: "Do users who order only one cuisine type have higher 90-day retention than multi-cuisine users?"
