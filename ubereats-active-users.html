⚠️
Chicago: High Spend, Low Satisfaction
Chicago avg order value ($45.14) is 30% above the 5-city mean ($37.08), yet its rating (3.50) is 0.43 stars below Miami's. Higher price raised expectations — experience didn't follow.
⚡
Promo Dependency — Test First
50% of top active orders used a promo code. Scaling a loyalty program on top of discount-driven behaviour risks training users to never pay full price.
📏
80 Rows = Direction, Not Decision
All findings are directional. Statistical significance requires 6–12 months of production data. Use this to form hypotheses, not commit budgets.

Ask Data with One Natural-language Question

SQL Generated by Claude
SELECT
  user_id,
  COUNT(*)                        AS order_count,
  ROUND(AVG(order_total), 2)      AS avg_spend,
  ROUND(AVG(rating), 1)           AS avg_rating,
  GROUP_CONCAT(DISTINCT cuisine_type
               ORDER BY cuisine_type
               SEPARATOR ', ')    AS cuisines_ordered
FROM ubereats_mock
GROUP BY user_id
HAVING order_count >= 2
ORDER BY order_count DESC
LIMIT 8;

Pattern: Top 3 users all ordered Burgers or Salads — no outlier cuisines. High-frequency behaviour clusters around familiar, low-decision categories.
Interpretation: Repeat ordering is habitual, not exploratory. Active users are not using the platform to discover new cuisines — they're reordering comfort foods.
