Multi-Touchpoint Attribution (MTA) · Real Advertising Data

Last-click method overvalued the channel by 89% and undervalued another by 42%.

“Last-click” refers that when running an ad campaign, it assigns 100% of conversion credit to the final touchpoint, while the earlier steps of awareness during the customer journey gets 0 credit. That easily brings the skewness for multi-touch campaigns and budget allocation efficiency. This article shows a comparison of two multi-touch attribution methods (MTA) against "Last-click". The data includes 16.4 million real ad impressions and 435,810 converting user journeys to measure how large that skew actually is.

0.66%

Only 0.66% of conversions crossed more than 1 channel. Most "multi-touch" paths were the same campaign shown repeatedly.

Note: Before you use MTA to check how credit is assigned, check if multi-touch affects your campaign first.

Baseline
Last-click

100% credit to the final touchpoint. The default setting in most ad platforms.

Algorithmic
Markov chain removal effect

Models the customer journey as a network graph. The contribution of a channel depends on how much the conversion probability drops when it's removed.

Algorithmic
Shapley value

Game-theoretic based concept. The contribution of a channel depends on the average marginal performance contribution of that channel compared with the one of all possible "orders of entry".
